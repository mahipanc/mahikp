About Me

From a young age, I have been drawn to both caring for others and understanding how the world works. My earliest experiences with medicine came from helping care for my grandmother. Living with her showed me that health is not only biological, but also deeply personal and cultural. Her preferences, beliefs, and understanding of treatment often differed from my own Western-rooted biomedical ideas. At first, those differences surprised me; however, they taught me one of the most important lessons: healing begins with empathy and understanding. As I continued caring for my grandmother through middle and high school, I learned that effective care requires more than knowledge of the disease itself. It requires communication, patience, and respect for each patient to understand how they see their illness and recovery. I became interested in medicine while also understanding the social forces shaping health, leading me to study Neuroscience and minor in Medical Anthropology and Chemistry at UNC Chapel Hill, where I plan to graduate in 2029. Through my coursework, I have uncovered hidden disparities and systemic inequalities throughout healthcare, strengthening my dedication to fair, equitable, and culturally responsive care. Ultimately, I aspire to become a physician who uses scientific discovery and social awareness to provide patients with truly comprehensive care.

In addition to caregiving, dance has deeply influenced how I connect with others and understand the value of support. I was drawn to dance by the sense of teamwork, where every performer relies on one another for support, collaboration, and trust, influencing my thoughts on care and community. Through my Medical Anthropology classes, I have learned that many people facing illness often do not have strong systems of support, whether because of social isolation, structural barriers, or unequal access to resources. Seeing those realities made me realize that I want to be part of the team that helps support patients back to health. Effective healthcare depends on people who work together to help others heal.

At the same time, years of practicing, performing, choreographing, and teaching strengthened my leadership, organization, and adaptability. Teaching dancers ranging from young children to older adults taught me how to communicate clearly, adjust to different learning styles, and create an encouraging environment where everyone could succeed. Whether building confidence in a beginner or helping a group work together under pressure, I learned how to meet people where they are and help them move forward, something I hope to bring to every future patient I serve.

Another lifelong interest of mine has been space and exploration. As a child, I frequently visited the planetarium and spent nights using my telescope to observe the moon and anything else I could find. I was fascinated by discovery and the possibility of where humanity could go next. Much later, after speaking with Dr. Bowles and Dr. Hammond, my perspective expanded. Rather than focusing on destinations, I became interested in how space travel impacts the human body. Specifically, understanding how microgravity and cosmic radiation affect human health. Now, as a prospective research intern in Dr. Bowles’ lab, I hope to study how these conditions impact the cardiovascular system and plasma. This work excites me because it combines medical advancements with safer and more sustainable space travel.

Looking ahead, I hope to continue building the skills needed to serve others through medicine. In the summer of 2026, I plan to get my EMT certification while shadowing physicians in private optometry and family medicine clinics. Through these experiences, I hope to gain hands-on clinical exposure and a deeper understanding of patient care across specialties, furthering my journey of discovery.
