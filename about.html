About Me

Curiosity has always guided my understanding of the world, whether observing the patterns of dance or the ways neurons coordinate the brain and body. My interest in medicine and culture began at home, by helping care for my granda. Living with her allowed me to see how perceptions of medicine and therapy can differ across generations and cultural backgrounds. I observed how her preferences, beliefs, and understanding of treatments sometimes differed from our own, and learned the importance of empathy, communication, and respect for her perceptions when navigating those differences. These experiences inspired me to study how culture influences health and recovery, leading me to minor in Medical Anthropology and Chemistry with a major in Neuroscience at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. Through my classes, I have uncovered hidden disparities and systemic inequalities that often go unnoticed, reinforcing my understanding of fair, equitable, and culturally responsive care. Together, these fields form the foundation for my aspiration to become a physician who bridges scientific rigor with social awareness. This interdisciplinary perspective also takes shape in my experience with dance, which has shaped my understanding of the connection between mind and body, as well as the importance of collaboration and resilience. Through years of training and performing, I have learned to notice subtle patterns in movement, respond to unexpected changes, and coordinate seamlessly with others. Teaching and choreographing further strengthened my ability to guide a group toward a shared goal, emphasizing organization, adaptability, and mentorship. Dance has shown me that physical practice is intertwined with cognitive, emotional, and social processes, where what the brain intends, the body expresses, and the community feels all align through movement. These experiences also translate directly to research and clinical settings, where teamwork, careful observation, and adaptability are essential to both scientific discovery and patient care. At the same time, my fascination with space has guided my curiosity toward human physiology under extreme conditions, linking my academic and personal interests in a meaningful way. Since childhood, I have been captivated by space exploration and the challenge of understanding how we adapt beyond Earth. This interest has inspired me to pursue research in space medicine, and I hope to join Dr. Bowles’ lab at Duke University to study how microgravity and cosmic radiation influence the cardiovascular system and plasma. This work would allow me to explore how the body adapts to prolonged spaceflight, connecting neuroscience, cardiovascular physiology, and human resilience in an applied context. Understanding these adaptations not only finds strategies to protect astronauts’ health during long-duration missions but also provides insights into recovery and adaptation in clinical populations on Earth. Together with my experiences in community outreach and educational initiatives, these opportunities reinforce my vision of becoming a physician who integrates biological science with cultural understanding, translating research into interventions that are effective, equitable, and accessible to all.
