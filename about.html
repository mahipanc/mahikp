About Me

I am a neuroscience major minoring in Medical Anthropology and Chemistry at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, graduating in 2029. I have been interested in medicine since I was young, starting when I helped take care of my grandma. Living with her allowed me to see how perceptions of medicine differ across generations and cultures, and how her preferences, beliefs, and understanding of treatments often differed from my own Western biomedical-based ideas. As I continued taking care of her through middle and high school, I further noticed these differences, learning the importance of empathy, communication, and respect for her perceptions and ideals, especially when addressing her diagnosis, treatment, and recovery. This lesson inspired me to study how culture influences health and recovery, pushing me to minor in Medical Anthropology. Throughout my classes, I have found many hidden disparities and systemic inequalities that reinforce my understanding of fair, equitable, and culturally responsive care. I aspire to become a physician who bridges scientific discovery with social awareness, providing my patients with comprehensive care.

A lot of my understanding of the body comes from dance. Years of practicing, performing, and choreographing/teaching have shaped the way that I think about movement. In dance, the same choreography and movements can look completely different based on timing, control, and body awareness, which made me curious about how my brain coordinates my muscles and learns through repetition. Teaching and choreographing also strengthened my leadership, organization, and adaptability, as I led young children to older adults.

Since childhood, I have also always been interested in space and space travel. I constantly visited the planetarium, watching shows about galaxies and black holes. I had a telescope at home through which I would observe the moon and anything else I could find. I was drawn to the idea of exploration and discovery. However, after speaking with Dr. Bowles and Dr. Hammond, I began thinking about space differently. Instead of focusing on where we could go, I became interested in how space travel affects the human body. Now, as a prospective research intern in Dr. Bowles' lab, I am focused on how microgravity and cosmic radiation impact the cardiovascular system and plasma. This work excites me because of its real-world application in making space travel safer and more sustainable.

To pursue my career and gain experience, I will be working toward my EMT certification and shadowing physicians in private optometry and family medicine clinics in the summer of 2026, gaining hands-on clinical exposure and a deeper understanding of patient care across specialties.
