Research

Microgravity and cosmic radiation can place significant stress on the human body, particularly the cardiovascular system, by disrupting normal circulation and cellular function during spaceflight. My research focuses on whether exposure to these spaceflight conditions affects cardiovascular health and plasma biomarkers associated with inflammation, vascular damage, and physiological stress. Through work in Dr. Bowles’ lab, I hope to better understand these changes in order to support safer long-duration missions and develop broader medical applications on Earth.

Lab Description

The Bowles Lab at Duke University focuses on understanding how spaceflight stressors, particularly microgravity and galactic cosmic radiation, impact cardiovascular health. Through the use of cell and animal models, the lab examines how these conditions influence molecular pathways related to cardiac function, remodeling, and disease development. This research supports the creation of protective strategies for astronauts on long-duration missions while also advancing knowledge of cardiovascular disease on Earth.
